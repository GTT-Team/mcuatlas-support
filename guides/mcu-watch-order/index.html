Guide 02 / Get oriented

MCU release order vs. timeline order

My recommendation: Start with Iron Man (2008) if this is your first Marvel Cinematic Universe watch. Start with Captain America: The First Avenger (2011) if you’re rewatching by when the main story takes place.

Release order follows when films and series debuted. Timeline order follows when their main stories take place. A curated route follows a specific goal, such as catching up for an Avengers film or following one character. None of these is a test of whether you’re a real fan.

Release order: the best first watch

Release order lets introductions, callbacks and post-credit scenes land when audiences first saw them. Here’s the Phase One starter run:

  1. Iron Man (2008)
  2. The Incredible Hulk (2008)
  3. Iron Man 2 (2010)
  4. Thor (2011)
  5. Captain America: The First Avenger (2011)
  6. The Avengers (2012)

It’s worth seeing The Avengers before wandering into later crossovers: this is where Iron Man, Captain America, Thor, Hulk, Black Widow and Hawkeye become a team. If you want to follow the Infinity Stones and Thanos through to the end of their main arc, continue through the Infinity Saga and keep Infinity War before Endgame.

Timeline order: better for a rewatch

The main story of The First Avenger is set during World War II, even though the film came out three years after Iron Man. Captain Marvel is primarily set in the 1990s, so it also moves ahead of Iron Man on a timeline rewatch. A practical opening sequence is The First Avenger → Captain Marvel → Iron Man.

Use the main setting as a rule of thumb. Flashbacks, framing scenes and post-credit teasers often fall elsewhere, and cutting films apart to place every scene in sequence usually makes a worse viewing experience.

When to use a focused route instead

  • Catching up for a specific release: use the Doomsday refresher instead of committing to every phase.
  • Following Wanda and Vision: keep WandaVision, Agatha All Along and VisionQuest in that sequence.
  • Watching across alternate universes: keep the films or seasons within each series in their own order first. An alternate universe’s period setting is not a reason to insert it into the main MCU chronology.

Can you switch halfway through?

Yes. Pick one order for the next few titles and track what you finish, rather than trying to make two lists match perfectly. MCU Atlas separates watched history from saved Next Up places, so finishing a film and changing your route don’t have to mean starting over.
