MCU Atlas helps you explore MCU watch orders, playlists, and characters, track what you've already watched, and keep your own independent saved places — right on your device.

What the app does

MCU Atlas presents a reference catalog of MCU titles, episodes, and character appearances, and lets you mark what you've watched yourself. The catalog is built into the app, so browsing it and marking what you've watched do not need an internet connection. It does not stream or play video, and it does not check streaming availability in real time — "Where to watch" destinations are recorded in the catalog with their own source and verification date, and open the provider's own app or website.

Progress tracking

Marking something watched is always an explicit action you take. Opening a "Where to watch" destination never marks anything watched, for any provider. MCU Atlas does not import progress from a streaming provider's own history — there is no such integration.

When the catalog is updated, entries are designed to keep their identity so your progress carries forward, but this is a design goal rather than an absolute guarantee for every possible edit.

Spoiler choice

The first time you open the app, you choose between two equally weighted options before seeing any content — neither is preselected. If you'd rather see everything immediately, choose the "show everything" option. You can revisit this choice later in Settings.

Your progress and iCloud

MCU Atlas keeps your progress on your device and, when your device is signed in to iCloud, in Apple's CloudKit private database under your own Apple Account — never on a server operated by the developer. The app is usable without an iCloud account; it is then designed to keep your progress on the device only. We don't promise that your progress will appear on every device you own. See the Privacy page for details.

Catalog updates

MCU Atlas checks for a newer public catalog each time it opens, and again when it comes back to the foreground if about six hours have passed since its last check. You can also check yourself from About. An update comes from the developer's static host on Cloudflare, is checked, and is used the next time you open the app; your progress and spoiler choice stay as they are. If a check fails (for example, with no connection), the app keeps the catalog it already has. A check sends nothing about what you watch. See the Privacy page for what the host receives.

Provider links, other links, Handoff, and Spotlight

Provider links
"Where to watch" destinations open the streaming provider's own app or website. MCU Atlas does not add your progress to the link and does not track what happens after you leave the app. On Apple TV, a checked store or provider page the TV cannot open, such as a Rent or Buy page, is shown as a code to scan with your phone; nothing is opened, bought or sent from the Apple TV.
Search YouTube (iPhone, iPad, Mac, Apple Vision Pro and Apple TV)
The last option in "Where to watch" searches YouTube for the title you are viewing: a film's title and year, or a show's name and episode code. Those search words go to YouTube, and YouTube's own privacy practices apply. Your watch history and progress are not included, and the app does not read the results. On iPhone and iPad the search opens in an in-app Safari view. On Mac and Apple Vision Pro it opens in a web view inside the app that does not save cookies or website data to your device; you can follow YouTube's own links within it. On Apple TV, it shows a code, made on the Apple TV, that you can scan with your phone's camera to open the same search on your phone; nothing is opened or sent from the Apple TV. Apple Watch has no YouTube search.
Other links
Source, reference and community links open in your browser or in a Safari window, and can contain spoilers. On iPhone and iPad, a character or cast row's image-search button opens Google Images in a Safari window, using the names and title shown on that row. The Privacy page has the details.
Handoff
In the iPhone, iPad and Mac apps, with Handoff turned on, the title, route, or search you're viewing can be picked up by your own nearby iPhone, iPad or Mac signed into the same Apple Account. The Apple Watch, Apple TV and Apple Vision Pro apps do not use Handoff.
Spotlight
In the iPhone, iPad and Mac apps, Spotlight on your device is given public catalog titles and curated route names only — never your watch history, notes, or searches. The Apple Watch, Apple TV and Apple Vision Pro apps add nothing to Spotlight.

Report an issue

Something wrong, missing, or confusing? Send us a note and we'll take a look.

Report an Issue

This link only opens a draft email in your own mail app, addressed to us with a subject line already filled in. Nothing is ever sent automatically, and nothing is attached or included beyond what you choose to write. We receive whatever you send us.

Inside the app, the Report an Issue control prepares a draft you can review first, with the category, the app version, the catalog snapshot date and your message. Nothing is sent until you send it yourself. The Privacy page lists exactly what the draft includes.

Known limits

  • Progress tracking is manual; there is no automatic import from any streaming provider.
  • The app does not check live streaming availability — "Where to watch" entries are catalog-recorded, not looked up at runtime.
  • Catalog updates are designed to preserve your progress, but this is not an absolute guarantee in every case.
  • Progress is kept in your private iCloud database only while the device is signed in to iCloud; the app does not promise that your progress appears on every device you own.
  • MCU Atlas does not use Live Activities.
  • MCU Atlas is an unofficial fan reference. It is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or sponsored by Marvel or The Walt Disney Company.
